# Antibiotic prescribing in the Xiantao village-clinic trial

Xu M, Wang J, Zhang Z, Ge E, Zhu Y, Li J, Gong Y, Lu Z, Wei X, and Yin X reported this cluster-randomised trial in *The Lancet Regional Health – Western Pacific* in 2026, volume 71, article 101888. The study is registered as NCT06568432. [Publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2026.101888) · [PubMed record 42239972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42239972/) · [Trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06568432)

## What the comparison measures

Forty village clinics in Xiantao, Hubei, China, were assigned to usual care or a package built around combined C-reactive protein and serum amyloid A point-of-care testing. The package included an algorithm for prescribing, in-person clinician training, written guidelines, desk reminders, monthly feedback on prescribing, patient education and a test copayment paid to the clinician. Clinicians retained prescribing discretion. The estimate compares all eligible index consultations according to clinic assignment. Only 7.4% of intervention consultations used the test. [Intervention and control procedures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13227237/#sec2.4)

The primary outcome counted an antibiotic prescription at the initial visit for an acute respiratory infection. The authors defined an initial visit by the absence of a recorded consultation at that clinic in the preceding two weeks. The intervention operated for six months, while the prescribing outcome occurred during the visit itself. The casebook therefore records a zero-month outcome horizon. The event code `adverse` represents the study's goal of reducing prescribing at the population level. Individual prescription appropriateness was outside this outcome. [Outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13227237/#sec2.6)

The authors' adjusted risk ratio was **0.80**, with a 95% confidence interval from **0.78 to 0.82**, for the package versus usual care. The project checked the estimate in the first sentence of the second Results paragraph and in the primary intention-to-treat row of Table 2. The `reviewed` annotation records this check against the published text. Independent scientific adjudication, a pooled analysis and patient-specific recommendations are outside this example. [Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13227237/#sec3)
